# PRINCE2 Agile — AI Agent Reference

## Purpose

Use this document as a retrieval-oriented reference when an AI agent must reason about, explain, tailor, or apply the supplied **PRINCE2 Agile (2015)** guidance.

This is a source-bounded restructuring of the supplied manual. It removes publication noise, OCR/layout artefacts, duplicate navigation text, broken line wraps, figure-only references, and non-essential acknowledgements while preserving the concepts, decision rules, terminology, tailoring guidance, and project-management relationships needed for agent use.

## Source constraints

- Treat this document as representing the supplied **2015 first edition / 2018 third impression** only.
- Do not silently import later PRINCE2, Scrum, SAFe, Kanban, Lean, or other framework changes.
- If a user asks for current or later-edition guidance, verify it separately and distinguish it from this source.
- Preserve the source distinction between **PRINCE2 Agile** and generic **agile**.
- PRINCE2 Agile is intended for **projects**. Agile ways of working may also be used in business-as-usual (BAU) work, but PRINCE2 Agile itself is project-focused.

## Agent operating rules

When applying this reference, use the following rules in order:

1. **Confirm the work is a project.** Do not treat PRINCE2 Agile as the management method for routine BAU work.
2. **Blend PRINCE2 and agile.** Do not run PRINCE2 governance and agile delivery as unrelated parallel systems.
3. **Retain the PRINCE2 core.** All seven PRINCE2 principles apply; all seven themes must be addressed; all seven processes remain relevant. Tailor how they are applied rather than deleting them.
4. **Tailor to context.** Use the project environment, risk, uncertainty, delivery model, customer involvement, communication conditions, and agile maturity to determine the degree and style of agile use.
5. **Manage by exception.** Empower teams inside explicit tolerances and escalate forecast breaches rather than micro-managing delivery.
6. **Protect time and quality by flexing what is delivered.** Lower-priority scope and lower-priority quality criteria are the primary contingency, not extra time, extra cost, or reduced essential quality.
7. **Maintain continued business justification.** Reassess viability as scope, releases, benefits, risks, and learning evolve.
8. **Use frequent feedback and incremental delivery.** Prefer evidence from delivered products, demonstrations, releases, measures, and customer feedback over activity-only reporting.
9. **Prioritize continuously.** Requirements, features, and where appropriate quality criteria should be prioritized so that essential outcomes are protected.
10. **Distinguish detail change from baseline change.** Detail can often be handled dynamically; changes that alter baselined product descriptions or the project justification require governance.
11. **Use agile behaviours deliberately.** Transparency, collaboration, rich communication, self-organization, and exploration are conditions to monitor, not optional slogans.
12. **Do not average the Agilometer.** Assess each slider independently and respond to weak conditions explicitly.
13. **Do not assume one-to-one role equivalence.** In particular, do not automatically equate the senior user with a product owner or a team manager with a Scrum master.
14. **Keep management products fit for purpose.** Required information must exist, but its format may be lightweight, visual, verbal, electronic, or embedded in another product where appropriate.
15. **State uncertainty.** If the source does not define an exact mapping, threshold, formula, or procedure, say so rather than inventing one.

# 1. Core model

## 1.1 What PRINCE2 Agile is

PRINCE2 Agile describes how to configure and adapt PRINCE2 so that it can be used effectively with agile behaviours, concepts, frameworks, and techniques.

The source presents PRINCE2 and agile as complementary:

- PRINCE2 is strongest in **project direction and project management**.
- Agile is strongest in **product delivery**.
- PRINCE2 Agile combines them into a single project-management approach.

The intent is a **blend**, not coexistence as separate systems.

## 1.2 Agile is framework-agnostic

PRINCE2 Agile treats agile as a family of:

- behaviours
- concepts
- frameworks
- techniques

It does not prescribe one agile framework. The source discusses Scrum, Kanban, Lean, Lean Startup, DSDM/AgilePM, DevOps, SAFe, XP, FDD, Crystal, DAD, and ASD as examples of agile-related approaches with differing scopes.

## 1.3 Project versus BAU

### Project characteristics

Typical project characteristics include:

- temporary work
- a team assembled for the work
- difficult or non-routine change
- uncertainty
- project direction and management
- eventual transition of products into operational use

### BAU characteristics

Typical BAU characteristics include:

- ongoing work
- stable teams
- routine or repeatable work
- greater certainty
- continual product maintenance, enhancement, or improvement

**Agent rule:** if the work is ongoing product development without a project boundary, do not describe PRINCE2 Agile as the controlling method unless the work is explicitly being managed as a project.

# 2. PRINCE2 core retained in an agile context

## 2.1 Seven principles

All seven principles apply:

1. **Continued business justification**
2. **Learn from experience**
3. **Defined roles and responsibilities**
4. **Manage by stages**
5. **Manage by exception**
6. **Focus on products**
7. **Tailor to suit the project**

### Agile application of the principles

| Principle | Agile-oriented application |
| --- | --- |
| Continued business justification | Keep value, benefits, viability, MVP, and changing evidence connected to the business case. |
| Learn from experience | Use short feedback loops, customer involvement, retrospectives, inspect-and-adapt, and continual improvement. |
| Defined roles and responsibilities | Add or map agile delivery roles carefully without losing PRINCE2 accountability. |
| Manage by stages | Align management stages with releases and significant timeboxes; use shorter stages where uncertainty warrants tighter control. |
| Manage by exception | Use tolerances to enable empowerment; in agile contexts, scope often has more tolerance while time and cost are more constrained. |
| Focus on products | Prioritize and decompose product descriptions, requirements, quality criteria, and acceptance criteria so delivery can flex without losing control. |
| Tailor to suit the project | Use contextual assessment, including the Agilometer, to decide how much agile can safely and effectively be used. |

## 2.2 Seven themes

Every theme must be addressed throughout the project.

| Theme | Core question | Agile tailoring focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business case | Why? | Viability, value/benefits, ranges of delivery, MVP, early benefits, rapid validation of assumptions. |
| Organization | Who? | Self-organizing teams, agile delivery roles, customer representation, role mapping, empowerment and management by exception. |
| Quality | What? | Separate scope from quality; protect fitness for purpose; integrate acceptance criteria, quality criteria, definition of done/ready, frequent review and testing. |
| Plans | How? How much? When? | Use empirical and collaborative planning, backlogs, fixed timeframes, release planning, and appropriate high-level plans. |
| Risk | What if? | Retain explicit risk management while recognizing both agile risk-reduction mechanisms and risks introduced by weak agile conditions. |
| Change | What is the impact? | Embrace detailed change; distinguish it from baseline change; design baselines and product descriptions so useful change can occur safely. |
| Progress | Where are we now? Where are we going? Should we carry on? | Track delivered products, quality, velocity/flow/value, releases, and evidence rather than relying mainly on time and cost consumed. |

### Theme rule

Some themes may appear more prominent in day-to-day agile delivery, particularly plans and progress, but **all seven remain important**.

## 2.3 Seven processes

The seven PRINCE2 processes remain the project-management process model:

1. **Starting up a project**
2. **Directing a project**
3. **Initiating a project**
4. **Controlling a stage**
5. **Managing product delivery**
6. **Managing a stage boundary**
7. **Closing a project**

PRINCE2 Agile integrates agile activities and artefacts into these processes rather than replacing the processes.

### Typical agile artefacts/events by process

| Process area | Typical agile artefacts or events identified in the source |
| --- | --- |
| Starting up a project | Vision, product roadmap |
| Initiating a project | Product backlog |
| Controlling a stage | Releases, release backlog, release retrospective |
| Managing product delivery | Sprints, sprint backlog, sprint review, retrospective |
| Managing a stage boundary | Release/stage evidence; similar delivery information to controlling a stage |
| Closing a project | Project retrospective |
| Directing a project | Governance and decision-making across the project; no single delivery artefact is mandated by the source table |

# 3. What to fix and what to flex

## 3.1 Six project aspects

PRINCE2 identifies six aspects to control:

- time
- cost
- quality
- scope
- benefits
- risk

PRINCE2 Agile provides tolerance guidance for deciding what should normally be fixed and what may flex.

## 3.2 Fix/flex tolerance guidance

| Aspect | Source guidance | Default treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | Zero tolerance for extra time on all levels of plan. | **Fix** |
| Cost | Zero tolerance for extra cost on all levels of plan. | **Fix** |
| Quality | Essential customer quality expectations, acceptance criteria, and essential quality criteria have zero tolerance; desirable criteria may have tolerance. | **Fix and flex** |
| Scope | Essential products have zero tolerance; desirable products may have tolerance. | **Fix and flex** |
| Benefits | Zero tolerance below the minimum-viability level in the business case; tolerance may exist above minimum viability. | **Fix or flex** |
| Risk | Tolerance depends on the specific project and is set by the project board/project manager as appropriate. | **Fix or flex** |

### Interpretation rule

“Fixed” does not mean an aspect can never change. It means tolerance is normally set to zero, so a forecast breach creates an exception requiring management attention.

## 3.3 Protect quality by flexing delivery

PRINCE2 Agile explicitly avoids meeting deadlines by compressing testing, assurance, documentation, design quality, training, or compliance activities that are necessary to meet the required level of quality.

Prefer to:

- remove or defer lower-priority scope
- relax lower-priority quality criteria where the source permits this
- retain essential acceptance/quality criteria
- preserve the required fitness for purpose

**Do not equate “flex quality criteria” with “reduce the required quality level.”**

## 3.4 Five targets behind flexing what is delivered

1. **Be on time and hit deadlines.**
2. **Protect the level of quality.**
3. **Embrace change.**
4. **Keep teams stable; do not add people merely to try to go faster.**
5. **Accept that the customer does not need everything.**

These targets are interdependent. The core trade is that the customer accepts that some lower-priority content may be omitted so that deadlines, essential quality, and responsive change can be protected.

## 3.5 Stable teams

Avoid adding people to a team as a short-term recovery tactic for difficult work. The source highlights several likely costs:

- onboarding time
- more communication paths
- opportunity cost to the area providing new people
- disruption of team dynamics

This guidance is especially relevant over short delivery periods such as a sprint. It does not prohibit planned team changes across the life of a project.

## 3.6 Trading and dynamic change

**Trading (swapping)** is the replacement of one or more requirements/features/user stories with others of similar effort.

Use this for detailed change where the higher-level baseline and project justification remain intact.

Escalate through change control where change affects:

- baselined product descriptions
- the project product description
- business justification
- tolerances
- the viability of the project

# 4. Five PRINCE2 Agile behaviours

Monitor these behaviours throughout the project.

## 4.1 Transparency

Desired condition:

- relevant information is open and visible
- bad news is visible early
- honesty, trust, integrity, and respect support the information flow
- progress and problems are not hidden

Transparency is more than display; it depends on behaviour.

## 4.2 Collaboration

Desired condition:

- people work as one team rather than in silos
- customer and supplier work with each other rather than at arm’s length
- team members help one another
- stakeholders are actively engaged
- goals and outputs have shared ownership

## 4.3 Rich communication

Desired condition:

- use the most effective communication channel available
- prefer face-to-face interaction and visualization where practical
- use prototypes, models, boards, information radiators, and direct conversation to improve speed and shared understanding
- retain documentation where needed, but do not default to documents when richer communication is better

## 4.4 Self-organization

Desired condition:

- people closest to the work decide how best to perform it within agreed boundaries
- teams own the plans they create
- management sets direction and tolerances without micro-managing delivery
- empowerment is supported by clear accountability

## 4.5 Exploration

Desired condition:

- use iteration and rapid feedback to learn
- seek feedback rather than waiting for it
- use experiments, prototypes, spikes, and other learning techniques to reduce uncertainty
- shorten feedback loops so errors are smaller and learning occurs sooner

### Behaviour monitoring

The source suggests a simple RAG-style dashboard:

- **Green** — behaviour is working satisfactorily
- **Amber** — concern exists and attention is needed
- **Red** — immediate action is needed

# 5. Business case and value

## 5.1 Continued justification

The business case is mandatory in PRINCE2 and remains central in PRINCE2 Agile.

The agent should continuously ask:

> How does this affect the business case?

Do not treat the approved business case as static. Update and revalidate it as learning, releases, risks, benefits, costs, and expected delivery change.

## 5.2 Flexible business case

When the amount delivered can vary, the business case may express:

- best-case delivery
- expected delivery
- worst-case delivery

The worst case still needs to leave the project viable.

## 5.3 Minimum viable product (MVP)

The business case should identify the MVP where relevant and may indicate when it is expected to be delivered or placed into operational use.

A forecast that the MVP cannot be delivered within its tolerance may create an exception.

**Do not confuse MVP with project viability.** The project must remain viable throughout its life; MVP is a product/delivery concept used within that wider business justification.

## 5.4 Benefits, value, output, and outcome

Use the following distinctions from the source:

- **Output** — what the project creates.
- **Outcome** — the result produced by using the output.
- **Benefit** — the measurable improvement resulting from the outcome.
- **Value** — an agile term often used alongside benefits; the source notes they are related but not identical.

Prefer decisions that maximize benefits/value rather than merely maximizing output volume.

Measures influence behaviour. Select measures that reflect the value actually sought.

# 6. Organization and role mapping

## 6.1 Governing principle

PRINCE2 accountabilities remain. Agile delivery roles may be added, mapped, merged, or tailored, but do not erase project-level accountability.

The project board represents business, user, and supplier interests and remains accountable for project success.

## 6.2 Project board

The project board needs to understand:

- not everything may be delivered
- the intended degree of agile use
- using either too much or too little agile for the environment may introduce risk
- PRINCE2 and agile operate as a coherent blend
- self-organization requires clear boundaries and management by exception
- organizational agile maturity influences how the project should be managed

## 6.3 Executive

The executive owns the business perspective and business justification. In agile contexts, the executive should understand the relationship between benefits and value.

The source notes that this role broadly maps to what some agile environments call a **sponsor**.

## 6.4 Senior user

The senior user:

- represents higher-level customer/user interests
- is ultimately responsible for the appropriateness of the delivered product from the user perspective
- is ultimately responsible for prioritization to maximize value and customer goals

Do **not** automatically map the senior user to the common product owner role. In some circumstances the senior user may function more like a “super product owner.”

## 6.5 Product owner

The source describes the product owner as a powerful delivery-level customer role, especially where one team owns one product.

Typical responsibilities include:

- expressing backlog items clearly
- ordering the backlog
- optimizing value
- maintaining backlog visibility and clarity
- helping the delivery team understand backlog items
- representing the customer view at a detailed level

In a project with multiple teams, products, stakeholder groups, or customer perspectives, one product owner may be insufficient to represent the full customer view.

## 6.6 Customer subject matter expert (SME)

The PRINCE2 Agile customer SME can provide the detailed customer voice inside a delivery team and may, where appropriate, be called a product owner if that terminology is already understood.

## 6.7 Business analyst / requirements specialist

The source identifies a possible specialist role—such as a business analyst or requirements engineer—to bridge customer and supplier perspectives.

Possible responsibilities include:

- translating terminology across customer/supplier groups
- gathering and defining requirements/user stories
- defining quality and acceptance criteria
- supporting customer-side testing
- challenging prioritization where necessary
- developing models and supporting documentation
- facilitating workshops
- supporting business-case information
- aligning requirements to strategic or project objectives
- liaising across senior user, customer SMEs, and stakeholders

## 6.8 Scrum master and team manager

A Scrum master facilitates/coaches the delivery process and helps remove impediments. The source says there is no exact PRINCE2 equivalent.

A PRINCE2 project using agile still needs a team manager **or equivalent accountability** for delivering a team’s products to the project manager.

Do not perform a simplistic Scrum master = team manager substitution without checking the responsibilities actually needed.

## 6.9 Working agreements

Self-organizing teams may create working agreements or team norms covering values, communication, meeting practices, escalation, and ways of working.

Good working agreements:

- are created collaboratively
- are visible
- evolve through retrospectives and learning
- support effectiveness rather than creating bureaucracy
- distinguish guidance from mandatory quality/definition-of-done constraints where appropriate

# 7. Quality

## 7.1 Scope is not quality

Treat scope and quality as different dimensions.

A project may flex lower-priority scope while still protecting the required level of quality and fitness for purpose.

## 7.2 Product descriptions

Product descriptions are mandatory but flexible in form. They may be represented using epics or user stories where the required product-description information is still satisfied.

Higher-level product descriptions may be formally baselined while lower-level descriptions may be maintained more informally.

## 7.3 Agile quality concepts

Useful concepts named by the source include:

- acceptance criteria
- quality criteria
- definition of done
- definition of ready
- test-driven development (TDD)
- behaviour-driven development (BDD)
- refactoring
- technical debt

Quality checking and testing should be integrated into delivery rather than deferred until the end.

## 7.4 Technical debt

The source uses technical debt as a metaphor for consequences of poor design, architecture, or development that create future work.

Where technical debt threatens tolerances or quality, treat it as a project-control concern rather than invisible delivery detail.

# 8. Plans and progress

## 8.1 Planning orientation

Agile planning often asks:

> How much can be delivered within a fixed timeframe?

This contrasts with planning primarily around how long a fixed amount of scope will take.

## 8.2 Planning forms

Use planning mechanisms appropriate to the level:

- project and stage plans for governance and management horizons
- release plans for incremental delivery
- backlogs for prioritized work
- sprint/timebox plans for near-term delivery
- visible low-tech formats where they improve communication
- Gantt-style planning only where it adds value at higher levels and does not duplicate backlog information

## 8.3 Progress evidence

Prefer evidence such as:

- products completed to agreed quality
- releases completed
- benefits/value enabled
- velocity
- lead/cycle time
- burn-up/burn-down information
- working product demonstrations
- acceptance evidence

Time and cost remain controlled, but they are not sufficient indicators of delivery progress.

## 8.4 Forecasting

Use actual delivery evidence to forecast future delivery where conditions remain reasonably comparable.

Do not present velocity as a universal productivity measure or as guaranteed future performance; the source uses it as an empirical rate under similar conditions.

# 9. Risk and change

## 9.1 Risk

Agile practices can reduce some risks through:

- frequent delivery
- customer interaction
- rapid feedback
- iterative/incremental work
- prioritization
- experimentation/prototyping
- self-organizing teams

Agile also creates or exposes specific risks, especially when there is weak:

- customer engagement
- empowerment
- collaboration
- communication
- agile understanding
- environmental support

Use explicit project risk management as well as agile techniques.

## 9.2 Detail change versus baseline change

### Detail change

A change that refines a requirement without changing the relevant higher-level product description can usually be handled dynamically through prioritization and trading.

### Baseline change

A change that alters a baselined product description, project product description, or justification requires the appropriate governance and may trigger change control or exception handling.

The source does not treat normal refinement of detailed requirements as uncontrolled “scope creep.”

# 10. Management products and information

## 10.1 General rule

PRINCE2 management products still need to exist in some form, but they do **not** all need to be separate formal documents.

They may be:

- formal documents
- emails
- conversations captured elsewhere
- dashboards
- information radiators
- burn charts
- slides
- spreadsheets
- data in information systems
- wall charts
- notes of meetings

They may be combined or split where that improves usability and still satisfies their purpose.

## 10.2 Product categories

The source uses three management-product categories:

- **Baseline products** — subject to change control.
- **Records** — dynamic information about project progress and state.
- **Reports** — snapshots used for communication and decision-making.

## 10.3 Selected agile tailoring guidance

### Business case

Allow flex in the amount delivered, identify MVP where appropriate, and make viability visible if lower-priority scope is not delivered.

### Change control approach

Account for frequent versions, expected change, and the distinction between detailed change and baseline change.

### Communication management approach

Support rich, frequent, and potentially informal communication while retaining necessary stakeholder information flows.

### Plans

Synchronize management plans with releases, timeboxes, and delivery-level plans.

### Project product description

Keep the ultimate output and outcome clear and visible so iterative delivery does not drift away from the project purpose. High-level composition may map to product groups, epics, or similar groupings with priorities.

### Quality management approach

Explicitly protect the required quality level and include agile quality practices and behaviours.

### Risk management approach

Include risks associated with using agile; pay particular attention to breakdowns in the behaviours that enable agile work.

### Work package

Treat the work package as a formal boundary that can still be collaboratively negotiated. It may include one or more sprints/releases. Define it tightly enough to preserve control and loosely enough for the delivery team to self-organize inside it.

### Daily log

May be used to capture significant information arising from informal communication.

### Issue / risk / lessons / quality registers and logs

These may be lightweight, visual, or low-tech where appropriate, provided the information remains usable and controlled.

### Checkpoint report

May be represented by an information radiator, burn chart, or—by agreement—delivery-team events where the needed information is transparent and can be pulled by the project manager.

### Highlight report

May include release and benefit information and may be verbal or visual where the information is transparent and accessible to the project board.

### End stage report

May be built incrementally through the stage and may draw on release reviews.

### Exception report

A forecast that the expected amount of delivery will fall outside tolerance may trigger exception handling. Burn charts or other delivery evidence can support that forecast.

# 11. The Agilometer

## 11.1 Purpose

Use the Agilometer to assess the project environment and answer:

> How agile can we be on this project?

It is a **guide for informed judgement**, not an automatic decision engine.

## 11.2 Six sliders

Assess each independently:

1. **Flexibility on what is delivered**
2. **Level of collaboration**
3. **Ease of communication**
4. **Ability to work iteratively and deliver incrementally**
5. **Advantageous environmental conditions**
6. **Acceptance of agile**

### Critical rule

Do **not** calculate an average score and treat it as an overall result. Averages can hide a critical weak condition.

## 11.3 Level-5 characteristics

### Flexibility on what is delivered

- stakeholders accept that detail will change
- significant changes remain controlled
- priorities are explicit
- flexing delivery is understood as a way to protect deadline and quality
- de-scoping/prioritization is collaborative but customer driven

### Level of collaboration

- one-team culture
- customer/supplier partnership
- high trust
- low blame
- minimal silo behaviour
- people actively help one another

### Ease of communication

- communication-rich environment
- strong face-to-face interaction where practical
- visible information, prototypes, and models
- easy access to relevant current and historical information
- low reliance on formal reporting when richer methods work better

### Ability to work iteratively and deliver incrementally

- partial delivery can provide value
- products and understanding can be refined through feedback
- experimentation and learning are normal
- “think big; start small” is supported
- incremental delivery creates real feedback and confidence

### Advantageous environmental conditions

- stable, appropriately skilled personnel
- people are sufficiently dedicated to the project
- suitable tools and communication platforms exist
- third parties can work in an agile way
- commercial, contractual, and compliance arrangements do not unnecessarily block agile delivery

### Acceptance of agile

- stakeholders understand agile behaviours, concepts, and techniques
- people have suitable training/experience
- stakeholders prefer or positively support the way of working
- peripheral functions such as procurement or quality assurance are agile-aware enough not to become avoidable blockers

## 11.4 Responding to weak sliders

Examples from the source include:

- challenge excessive “must” priorities
- educate stakeholders about why delivery needs flexibility
- remove collaboration barriers
- improve co-location or remote collaboration mechanisms
- identify an early useful release
- protect teams from unnecessary disturbance
- improve tooling or training
- align commercial arrangements with agile working
- use senior stakeholders to explain agile expectations

## 11.5 Reassessment

Revisit suitability throughout the project, including after releases or at stage boundaries. If actual conditions differ materially from expectations, adjust project management and delivery controls.

If conditions become too risky for the intended degree of agile use, reduce or change the approach and use PRINCE2 exception mechanisms where required.

# 12. Requirements

## 12.1 Terminology and decomposition

The source uses product-oriented terminology at different levels of detail.

| Approximate level | Preferred PRINCE2 Agile terms | Possible equivalents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest | Project product description | Vision |
| High | Product group; project product description composition; high-level requirement | Epic, feature, function, theme, scope |
| Intermediate | Product description; intermediate-level requirement | Epic, feature, function, coarse-grained user story |
| Low | Product description; sub-product; low-level/detailed requirement | User story, fine-grained user story |

Requirements may be:

- **functional** — what the product does
- **non-functional** — how well it does it

Tasks used to fulfil requirements are not themselves treated as requirements.

## 12.2 Product-based planning

Product-based planning emphasizes the required end state of products/sub-products rather than planning only by activity.

Use product definitions and acceptance/quality criteria to make “done” explicit.

## 12.3 Prioritization

Prioritization is central to the PRINCE2 Agile approach because it enables deadlines and essential quality to be protected.

The source describes two common techniques:

- **MoSCoW** — normally useful at higher levels, over longer timeframes, and where groupings/dependencies matter.
- **Ordering (1..n)** — normally useful for lower-level items that are more independent.

### MoSCoW

Always apply MoSCoW relative to a deadline or timebox.

| Priority | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Must have | Without it, the timebox output will not work or is not worth delivering. |
| Should have | Highly desirable or important, but not essential. |
| Could have | Desirable or useful, but less important than a should. |
| Won’t have for now | Will not be satisfied before the relevant deadline. |

### Test a “must”

Ask whether the product would still be viable for operational use without the requirement. If yes, challenge whether it is genuinely a must.

### Decompose “musts”

A high-level must may contain lower-level musts, shoulds, and coulds. Decomposition can expose contingency without changing the essential high-level outcome.

### Do not deliver all musts first by default

Dependencies and product coherence may require a sequence that includes shoulds or other work before some musts can be completed.

## 12.4 Prioritizing quality criteria

Where appropriate, quality criteria can also be prioritized. Essential criteria remain protected; lower-priority criteria may form part of delivery flexibility.

## 12.5 Requirement change

Use prioritization and trading for detailed change inside the agreed baseline.

Use governance for changes that alter baselined higher-level products, project purpose, or business justification.

# 13. Rich communication

## 13.1 Communication principle

Select the richest practical communication channel for the situation.

Prefer direct, visual, collaborative communication where it produces faster and clearer shared understanding.

## 13.2 Useful mechanisms

Depending on context, use:

- face-to-face conversation
- workshops
- whiteboards
- prototypes
- models
- information radiators
- visible plans/backlogs
- demonstrations
- collaborative planning
- video-conferencing when teams are distributed
- concise documentation where durable information or governance requires it

## 13.3 Documentation rule

Do not interpret agile as “no documentation.”

Reduce or replace documentation only where another communication mechanism better serves the need and the required information remains available, accurate, and controlled.

# 14. Frequent releases

## 14.1 Why release frequently

Frequent delivery can:

- enable early benefits
- create feedback
- reduce the risk of delivering the wrong product
- provide visible evidence of progress
- increase stakeholder engagement
- make release activity routine

## 14.2 Release planning

Release planning is a project-management concern, not only a delivery-team concern.

Project/stage planning should show, at an appropriate level:

- expected releases
- timing
- intended features or product content
- relationship to benefits and business justification

Synchronize release planning across project, stage, and delivery levels.

## 14.3 Operational release

The strongest feedback and benefit generally come when a release goes into operational use.

If operational release is not possible, a staging environment may still provide evidence of:

- progress
- quality
- integration
- customer feedback through demonstrations

However, staging can create rework, configuration/version-control complexity, and delayed real-world benefit.

## 14.4 Do not release faster than the organization can absorb

Release frequency should be agreed with affected stakeholders. Training, service/support, compliance, operations, and other customer constraints may limit a useful release cadence.

# 15. Creating contracts for agile work

## 15.1 Problem addressed

Traditional contracts often fix detailed requirements, price, and deadline even though detailed understanding changes during difficult project work.

PRINCE2 Agile recommends structuring commercial arrangements so they support collaboration, change, and value rather than turning expected change into avoidable conflict.

## 15.2 Contract guidelines from the source

Where appropriate:

1. Focus on **outcomes or throughput** rather than only fixed outputs.
2. Define the required level of **customer involvement**.
3. Buy **time/throughput in timeboxes** with defined deliverables.
4. Allow the project to end early if evidence shows it is no longer viable.
5. Relate incentives to value or throughput with care.
6. Avoid over-specifying detailed requirements at contract start.
7. Prioritize requirements and identify an MVP where relevant.
8. Handle detailed requirement change through trading.
9. Consider beginning with a minimum contract structure and adding clauses only where needed, if the trust/commercial context supports this.

## 15.3 Incentives and gaming

If incentives are linked only to throughput, they can be gamed—for example by inflating estimates or selecting easier work.

Outcome/value-based measures may reduce this risk but can be more difficult to define objectively.

## 15.4 Contract posture

The source’s intended posture is collaborative: the contract should support the relationship and act as a safety net rather than a weapon.

# 16. Process guidance by lifecycle point

## 16.1 Starting up and initiating

Ensure early alignment on:

- project purpose and vision
- business justification
- project product description
- initial roadmap/backlog
- approach to releases
- role/accountability structure
- degree of agile suitability
- quality expectations and acceptance
- how management controls will integrate with delivery practices

Avoid unnecessary detail where uncertainty is high; establish enough control to start safely and learn.

## 16.2 Directing a project

The project board should:

- govern by exception
- create clear decision boundaries
- understand the selected degree of agile working
- stay sufficiently engaged to make timely decisions
- use evidence from releases, benefits, risks, and tolerances
- avoid micro-managing delivery teams

## 16.3 Controlling a stage

The project manager should integrate stage control with delivery evidence from releases, backlogs, reviews, and team information.

Use transparency to pull needed status information rather than introducing reporting purely for administrative convenience.

## 16.4 Managing product delivery

Delivery teams should work within the work-package boundary using suitable agile techniques.

Key concerns include:

- self-organization
- prioritized work
- iterative development
- integrated quality
- reviews and retrospectives
- Kanban/flow or timeboxed working as appropriate
- MVP/experimentation where uncertainty warrants it

## 16.5 Managing a stage boundary

Use actual delivery evidence to support the project board’s decision to continue, change, or stop.

Frequent delivery should mean much of the needed information is already visible before the formal stage-boundary event.

## 16.6 Closing a project

Closure remains a formal project-management point. Agile delivery can make it smoother because:

- products may already be in operational use
- benefits may already be accruing
- quality/acceptance evidence has accumulated incrementally
- documentation should be close to complete

Confirm acceptance, outstanding actions, lessons, and closure rather than allowing a project to drift into BAU without a clear end.

# 17. Health-check heuristics for an AI agent

Use these as evidence prompts, not as a substitute for the source health check or formal PRINCE2 health check.

## 17.1 Behaviours

Look for evidence that:

- collaboration, trust, and no-blame behaviour exist
- self-organization is supported
- inspect-and-adapt and continual improvement are normal
- transparency is visible
- teams discuss benefits/value as well as output
- work and planning are collaborative
- stakeholders are kept informed
- the project board interacts with the project rather than remaining distant

## 17.2 Environment

Look for evidence that:

- teams are stable and protected enough to work effectively
- customer representatives are engaged and empowered
- the project is change-friendly
- the desired output and outcome are understood
- feedback is actively sought
- communication is fast and effective
- control is light-touch inside clear boundaries
- “musts” are genuinely essential
- frequent releases or meaningful increments occur
- the five fix/flex targets are understood
- roles are clear despite terminology differences
- agile is understood as a way of working, not merely a set of ceremonies

## 17.3 Decision red flags

Escalate or explicitly warn when you observe:

- practically everything is classified as mandatory
- deadlines are protected by reducing essential testing/quality
- time/cost overruns are treated as the first recovery mechanism
- extra people are added to a struggling team as a short-term default
- the business case is ignored after initiation
- the project board micro-manages delivery detail
- product-owner authority is unclear or represents too narrow a customer view
- agile roles erase mandated project accountability
- release plans are disconnected from project/stage plans
- formal reports duplicate transparent delivery information without adding decision value
- detailed requirement changes are treated as major change when the baseline is unaffected
- major baseline changes are hidden as “agile flexibility”
- Agilometer sliders are averaged into a single score
- all agile techniques are applied identically regardless of project context

# 18. Definitions and quick-reference terms

## Acceptance criteria

Criteria used to determine whether the project product or a requirement/user story is acceptable. In the source, acceptance criteria at the project-product level connect closely to the customer’s quality expectations.

## Backlog

A prioritized list of work or requirements. Product and sprint backlogs are common agile artefacts integrated into PRINCE2 Agile delivery.

## Definition of done

A shared set of criteria used to decide when work is complete to the required standard.

## Definition of ready

Criteria indicating that an item is sufficiently understood/prepared for work to start.

## Experiment

A structured investigation used to prove/disprove an idea or improve understanding or ways of working.

## Flow-based working

Work is pulled through a queue and states rather than partitioned primarily into low-level timeboxes.

## Minimum viable product (MVP)

The minimum product/delivery state used to create meaningful value or learning within the project context. Do not equate this automatically with the minimum viability of the business case.

## Release

A set of products handed over as a managed/tested/deployed entity. A release may contain multiple low-level timeboxes, though delivery into operational use may occur more frequently.

## Spike / spiking

Temporary work—such as research or a prototype—used to reduce uncertainty from a technical or customer perspective.

## Timebox

A finite period in which work is carried out toward a goal. The deadline should not move; work inside the timebox is prioritized.

## Trading / swapping

Replacing one or more requirements/features/user stories with others of similar effort to handle detailed change without increasing time/cost.

## Velocity

An empirical description of a team’s rate of delivery that may help forecast future delivery where conditions remain similar.

# 19. Agent response pattern

When a user asks for PRINCE2 Agile advice using this source, structure the reasoning as follows:

1. **Context** — project or BAU; stage/lifecycle position; delivery model; known constraints.
2. **Applicable PRINCE2 control** — principle, theme, process, role, product, or tolerance.
3. **Agile tailoring** — behaviour, technique, role mapping, release/backlog mechanism, or communication style.
4. **Fix/flex decision** — what is fixed, what may flex, and why.
5. **Business-case effect** — benefit/value/viability implication.
6. **Tolerance and escalation** — whether the issue remains within delegated control or creates an exception/baseline change.
7. **Recommended action** — smallest source-supported action that preserves governance and agile delivery.
8. **Evidence gap** — identify any project-specific facts needed before making a stronger recommendation.

Applicable PRINCE2 principle/theme/process:
Agile tailoring consideration:
Fixed constraints:
Flexible constraints:

Missing evidence / assumptions:
